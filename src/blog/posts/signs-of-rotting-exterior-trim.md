Exterior trim is the first part of a house to take a hit from the weather. On Cape Cod, wind-driven rain, salt air and freeze-thaw cycles find every joint, and once water gets behind a board it can soften the wood, and eventually the sheathing and framing underneath, without much showing on the surface.

The good news: trim rot almost always gives warning signs. Catching them early usually means a smaller repair instead of a bigger one.

## Why trim rots on Cape Cod homes

Most trim failures start at the same places: joints, end grain and anywhere water sits. Corner boards, window and door casings, fascia behind the gutters, rake boards and the bottom edges of trim near the ground take the most water.

A few things speed it up:

- **Wind-driven rain** pushes water sideways into joints that would stay dry in a calmer climate.
- **Paint failure** lets water into the wood, and bare end grain soaks it up quickly.
- **Overflowing or clogged gutters** keep fascia boards wet for days after a storm.
- **Missing flashing** above windows and doors sends water behind the casing instead of away from it.

## 7 signs your trim is rotting

### 1. Paint peeling or blistering at the joints

Paint that peels in the middle of a board is often just old paint. Paint that peels or bubbles at joints, corners and the ends of boards usually means moisture is getting into the wood from behind or from the end grain.

### 2. Dark staining or streaks

Grey or black staining, especially running down from a joint or a window head, shows where water is being held. Mildew lines along the bottom edge of a board are another tell.

### 3. Soft spots

Press a screwdriver or awl into the suspect area with light pressure. Sound wood resists. If the tip sinks in easily or the wood feels spongy, it has started to rot.

### 4. Open corners and gaps

Corner boards and mitered casings that have opened up let water straight in. Gaps also tend to grow: wet wood swells, dries, shrinks and opens the joint a little more each season.

### 5. Cupped, split or swollen boards

Boards that have curled at the edges, split along the grain or swollen out of line with the boards next to them have been taking on water for a while.

### 6. Sagging gutters or fascia pulling away

When fascia softens, gutter fasteners lose their grip. A gutter that sags, pulls away or overflows in one spot is often a sign of soft fascia behind it.

### 7. Signs of insects or animals at the soffits

Carpenter ants, bees and squirrels go for soft, damp wood and existing gaps. Activity around the soffits and rake boards is worth a closer look.

## What to do if you find rot

**Don't just caulk over it.** Caulk and paint can make a soft board look fine for a season, but they trap the moisture that caused the problem. The rot keeps spreading behind the finish.

**Find out how far it goes.** Trim rot often stops at the board, but sometimes the sheathing or framing behind it is soft too. The only way to know is to open it up. A good repair includes checking what's underneath and fixing it before new trim goes on.

**Repair or replace?** A small section of rot at the end of a long board can sometimes be cut out and spliced. When the board is soft over a large area, or the same spot has failed before, replacing it is usually the better long-term fix.

**Consider the material.** On exposed walls, many Cape Cod homeowners switch to PVC or cellular trim, which doesn't rot. Wood still works well where it's protected and well painted, and it may be the right choice on older homes where the look matters. Either way, the details matter more than the material: back-priming, sealed end grain, proper flashing above windows and doors, and joints that shed water.
