Cedar shingles are part of what makes a Cape Cod house look like a Cape Cod house. They're also one of the most common questions we get: is this wall fine, does it need a few shingles replaced, or is it time to re-shingle?

Here's how to tell, and what to expect from each option.

## Grey isn't the same as worn out

New white cedar starts out tan and weathers to silver-grey over a few seasons in the sun and salt air. That color change is normal and doesn't mean the shingles are failing. Plenty of grey walls have years of life left.

What matters is the condition of the shingle itself and what's happening behind it.

## Signs your shingles need attention

- **Cupping or curling.** Shingles that have curled at the edges no longer lie flat, so water can get underneath.
- **Splitting and cracking.** A few splits are normal. Many shingles split through, especially on the same wall, point to shingles that have dried out and become brittle.
- **Missing or loose shingles.** Often after a storm, or where fasteners have rusted through.
- **Rust streaks.** Staining below nail heads means the fasteners are corroding, which eventually lets shingles slip or fall.
- **Moss, rot and softness at the bottom courses.** The lowest rows near the ground, decks and roof lines stay wet the longest.
- **Water stains inside.** Stains on interior walls or around windows mean water is getting past the siding, and the problem is no longer only cosmetic.

On most houses, the south- and east-facing walls and the areas under windows and behind downspouts show wear first.

## When a repair makes sense

A repair is usually the right call when the damage is limited to one area, the shingles around it are still flexible and lie flat, and the sheathing behind is sound. We remove the damaged courses, check and fix what's underneath, and weave new shingles in.

New shingles will look lighter than the old ones at first. They weather toward the same grey over the next few seasons.

## When it's time to re-shingle

Replacement makes more sense when:

- Cupping, splitting or brittleness runs across most of a wall rather than one small area.
- Fasteners are failing across a large area.
- Water is getting behind the siding, or the sheathing underneath has gone soft.
- The same wall has needed repairs several times.

At that point, patching tends to cost more over time than doing the wall once, properly.

## What a proper cedar siding job includes

The shingles are only the part you see. A wall that keeps water out for decades also needs:

- **Sound sheathing.** Soft or rotted areas found during tear-off get repaired, not covered.
- **A weather-resistive barrier** behind the shingles.
- **Flashing** at window and door heads, sills, and where walls meet roofs and decks.
- **Corrosion-resistant fasteners**, stainless or hot-dipped galvanized, so they don't rust and stain.
- **Clean trim transitions** at corners, windows and doors.

## A note on historic districts

If your home is in the Old King's Highway Regional Historic District (along Route 6A in towns like Sandwich, Dennis and Orleans) or another local historic district, visible exterior changes may need approval before work starts. Cedar for cedar is usually straightforward, but it's worth checking first. We flag this at the estimate.
