Accessory Dwelling Units, or ADUs, have become one of the most-asked-about projects on Cape Cod since Massachusetts changed its rules in 2025. Whether you want space for aging parents, a place for adult kids, or rental income, here's what you need to know before you start.

## What is an ADU?

An ADU is a small, independent home on the same lot as your main house, with its own kitchen, bathroom and entrance. It can be:

- **A garage conversion**, often the most direct option since the foundation, walls and roof are already there.
- **An in-law suite** inside the existing house, including basement or attic units.
- **An attached addition** with its own entrance.
- **A detached backyard cottage.**

## What changed in Massachusetts

Since February 2025, state law allows homeowners to build one ADU **by right** in single-family zoning districts. In practice that means:

- **No special permit** is required for a qualifying ADU in those districts.
- **Size limit:** up to 900 sq ft, or half the size of the main home, whichever is smaller.
- **No owner-occupancy requirement** under state law.
- **A building permit and code compliance are still required.**

Towns can still set reasonable local rules on things like setbacks, site plan review and short-term rentals, so the details vary from town to town.

## What still applies on Cape Cod

The state law makes ADUs easier, but a few Cape-specific issues come up early in almost every project.

### Septic and Title 5

Most Cape Cod homes are on septic systems, and Massachusetts Title 5 sizes septic systems by the number of bedrooms. Adding a bedroom in an ADU usually means a review by the local Board of Health and may require upgrading the system. This is often the biggest factor in feasibility and budget, so it should be checked first.

### Historic districts

Homes along Route 6A in Sandwich, Barnstable, Yarmouth, Dennis, Brewster and Orleans fall in the Old King's Highway Regional Historic District, and towns like Provincetown have their own historic review. A garage conversion or new cottage visible from the street may need design approval.

### Wetlands, coast and flood zones

Projects near wetlands, coastal banks or in flood zones may involve the town's Conservation Commission and additional building requirements.

## What affects the cost

Every ADU is different, but the biggest cost drivers are usually:

- The type of ADU (conversion vs. new construction)
- Septic work, if needed
- Plumbing runs for the kitchen and bathroom
- Foundation and site work for detached cottages
- Finish level: cabinets, flooring, fixtures and trim
